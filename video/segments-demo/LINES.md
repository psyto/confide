## `01-a-stock-shut-the-way-the-real-ones-are.mp4` — a stock shut the way the real ones are

This is Confide on devnet, in a local demo where one presenter holds every key; chain waits are fast-forwarded, real time on the badge. The stock mint carries the two settings we found on all 1,992 measured equity mints: approval required, no auditor key.

## `02-confide-builds-the-proofs-and-checks-the-amount.mp4` — Confide builds the proofs and checks the amount

A new investor account waits for approval. Confide builds both legs' proofs, the chain verifies them, and the investor checks the amount before signing.

## `03-the-gate-holds.mp4` — the gate holds

The issuer sends the allocation; Token-2022 refuses it: the account isn't approved. The investor tries approving itself: refused. Only the mint's approval authority, here the issuer's key, can open it.

## `04-approval-then-one-transaction.mp4` — approval, then one transaction

The issuer approves exactly this account. With only the issuer's signature, the RPC rejects it; it never enters a block. With the investor's signature added, the same transaction settles: twenty thousand shares for three and a half million dollars, one atomic transaction that Confide assembled.

## `05-what-anyone-can-see.mp4` — what anyone can see

Accounts and transactions stay public; every balance here reads zero. On a public DEX, everyone would read this size; on a centralized exchange, the exchange would. Here, only the two parties' keys open it.

## `06-two-approved-holders-trade.mp4` — two approved holders trade

The investor offers five thousand shares for eight hundred seventy-five thousand dollars, terms agreed off chain. Confide pins them on each side before any proof exists; each side checks what it receives against its pin, and the second signer verifies the exact transaction. It settles atomically. With no mint-wide auditor key, the issuer's approval does not make it a reader of the amounts.

## `07-the-short-delivery-control.mp4` — the short-delivery control

Now the control. The issuer builds its leg for two thousand shares instead of twenty thousand; the proofs are valid, only the encrypted amount is wrong. Before any signature, the investor's Confide client decrypts the amount addressed to it, finds two thousand where twenty thousand was agreed, and refuses. Nothing is signed. Nothing moves.

## `08-what-confide-did.mp4` — what Confide did

Token-2022 and Solana enforce the rules; Confide builds the proofs, checks before signing, pins the terms, and assembles one transaction. Not a venue, no matching. Devnet only; no real issuer yet.
